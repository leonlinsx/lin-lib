Amarone is a full-bodied Italian red made mainly in Valpolicella, in the Veneto region. What makes it distinctive is the appassimento method: grapes are dried before fermentation, concentrating sugar, flavor, and texture.

For beginners, Amarone is useful because it shows how winemaking technique can dramatically shape style. It is typically dark, intense, and warming, with flavors of dried cherry, plum, spice, cocoa, and sometimes a slightly savory edge.

## At a glance

- **Region:** Valpolicella, Veneto, Italy
- **Typical style:** Full-bodied, rich, dry red
- **Key grapes:** Corvina, Corvinone, Rondinella
- **Good for:** Hearty meals, winter drinking, learning about appassimento
- **Similar to:** Powerful Rhône reds, some rich Rioja Gran Reserva

## Why it matters

Amarone is one of Italy’s best-known luxury wine styles and a clear example of how grape drying changes texture, alcohol, and flavor concentration.
